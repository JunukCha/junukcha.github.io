Abstract

Robot fingerspelling requires precise handshapes and rapid transitions for arbitrary words, yet paired word–action data for robot fingerspelling remains limited. We present RoboFS, a pipeline that generates human fingerspelling from input words, retargets it to a Shadow Hand while preserving bone directions and fingertip relations, and executes it in physics simulation with RoboFS-Tracker, a single reinforcement-learning tracking policy. Evaluated with a fingerspelling recognizer, RoboFS-Tracker achieves 77.4% letter accuracy, outperforming both inverse kinematics and open-loop replay baselines by over 25 percentage points. Penetration and action-rate penalties reduce penetration frames from 33.4% to 4.3% and fingertip jerk from 242 to 189 m/s3 while maintaining recognition accuracy. Using RoboFS, we generate RoboFS-Data with 50k words and train RoboFS-T2A to predict robot actions directly from input words. On unseen test words, RoboFS-T2A improves letter accuracy from 77.4% with RoboFS to 81.1% and reduces penetration frames from 4.3% to 2.6% (depth > 1 mm).

Method

Overview of RoboFS. Given a word, a text-conditioned generator synthesizes human fingerspelling landmarks. Relation-preserving retargeting constructs robot reference landmarks by preserving bone directions and fingertip relations. Conditioned on the simulated hand state and upcoming motion references, RoboFS-Tracker produces 18-dimensional actions and executes them in physics simulation. Recording its actions with the input word yields training pairs for RoboFS-Data.

Data Generation and Text-to-Action

RoboFS-Data and RoboFS-T2A. To build data at scale, a retargeting network replaces the retargeting optimization, and RoboFS-Tracker executes the predicted references for 50k training words. The recorded (word, robot action) pairs form RoboFS-Data, which supervises RoboFS-T2A. At inference, RoboFS-T2A predicts robot actions directly from an input word, without the generator, retargeting, or tracker.
